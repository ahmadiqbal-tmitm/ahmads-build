Why Build 52

Are we living through the last days of clicking?

"The best way to predict the future is to invent it." — Alan Kay

Earlier this month I left Canva after five-plus years. I was Canva's first GenAI product manager, and most recently General Manager for the Middle East, North Africa and Pakistan.

It was a busy, hectic, all-in kind of job. Evenings and weekends weren't the exception; they were the rhythm. Now I have something rare: time. So I'm diving in, head first, into agents.

Here's my bet: we're moving into a post-SaaS world.

Agents can do almost anything a human with software can do. Think about what that means. For decades, if you wanted a computer to do something, you had to learn its language: menus, buttons, tabs, shortcuts. Click here, scroll there, type this. Agents flip that around. Now the computer learns yours.

Elevators used to have operators. You'd step in, say "fifth floor, please", and a person made it happen. Then we swapped the operator for a panel of buttons and called it progress. Agents bring the operator back. Picture a software-less, UX-less future, where voice and speech beat clicking, navigating and typing as the way we make things happen and uncover insights.

So I'm building for 2028. Not for the world as it is, but for the one I think is coming.

To do that, I need to get warm and comfortable with this technology, hands on, in a way I never had the time for. At Canva I explored AI as a corporate knowledge worker: managing a team of humans, tracking our progress to goals. Useful, but it had a ceiling. Every use case was a Canva use case. Meanwhile there are millions of other tasks, in millions of other lives, that an agent could help with. I want to find them.

Enter Build 52: one agent a week, for a year, built in public.

Canva democratised design. It gave anyone, anywhere, the confidence to make something beautiful, no design degree required. Build 52 wants to do the same for agents: a marketplace of agents that anyone can install and get up and running with, as easily as possible. No code. No jargon. No computer science degree.

I spent five years building Creative AI for 250M people. Now I want to build AI Agents for a billion.

The first one is already live: a chief of staff that runs my mornings and guards my evenings. Read how I built it in week 1, or install your own at ahmads.build/agents/chief-of-staff.
